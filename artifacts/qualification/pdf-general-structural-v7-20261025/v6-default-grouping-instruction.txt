Extract all genuine structural headings in the document, at every level of its outline, including nested sections and subsections. Do not restrict selection to the highest-level headings.
A structural heading is a source occurrence that names, introduces, or divides a unit of document content at its actual location. It does not need to summarize that content in descriptive words.
A heading may span multiple source lines. A standalone structural label or number (such as "Chapter I") and a following descriptive title (such as "GENERAL PROVISIONS") may jointly form one heading. When the source context shows that they belong to the same heading, select every contributing source line and preserve its exact source alias. A standalone label may also function as a heading without a separate descriptive title.
Include genuine headings at all structural levels, including chapters, articles, sections, and lower-level subdivisions. Do not exclude an occurrence merely because it is subordinate to another heading.
Do not select numbered body paragraphs, table-of-contents references, cross-references, or other text merely because their wording, numbering, or appearance resembles a heading. Determine the structural function of each occurrence at its own location using its text, layout, and surrounding context.
When the available evidence cannot establish that function, request additional source evidence or report the occurrence as unresolved rather than guessing.
A source line can contain a heading together with body text. On a SELECT, give headingExtent when only part of the line is heading text: start and end are UTF-16 offsets into the exact target text, end exclusive, and text is exactly that substring. Omit headingExtent only when the whole source line is heading text. The program checks the span against the source; it never moves an extent or replaces an invalid one with the whole line.
Membership is already fixed. Divide selected source texts into individual headings.
A run means only that selected lines are adjacent; it is not a proposed heading.
Join lines when the later text continues the same title or supplies a contributing part
of that title. Keep independently named content units separate even when one immediately
contains the next: a section and its subsection, an agenda and its day divider, or a day
and its session each retain their own heading when they introduce different local units.
Related subject matter, consecutive placement, or a containing unit does not establish
title continuation. Use wording, labels, layout and the surrounding content together.
A genuine title wrapped over several lines still forms one heading. Preserve every selected
alias exactly once and decide boundaries only; do not add or remove heading membership.
