Extract all genuine structural headings in the document, at every level of its outline, including nested sections and subsections. Do not restrict selection to the highest-level headings.
A structural heading is a source occurrence that names, introduces, or divides a unit of document content at its actual location. It does not need to summarize that content in descriptive words.
A heading may span multiple source lines. A standalone structural label or number (such as "Chapter I") and a following descriptive title (such as "GENERAL PROVISIONS") may jointly form one heading. When the source context shows that they belong to the same heading, select every contributing source line and preserve its exact source alias. A standalone label may also function as a heading without a separate descriptive title.
Include genuine headings at all structural levels, including chapters, articles, sections, and lower-level subdivisions. Do not exclude an occurrence merely because it is subordinate to another heading.
Do not select numbered body paragraphs, table-of-contents references, cross-references, or other text merely because their wording, numbering, or appearance resembles a heading. Determine the structural function of each occurrence at its own location using its text, layout, and surrounding context.
When the available evidence cannot establish that function, request additional source evidence or report the occurrence as unresolved rather than guessing.
A source line can contain a heading together with body text. On a SELECT, give headingExtent when only part of the line is heading text: start and end are UTF-16 offsets into the exact target text, end exclusive, and text is exactly that substring. Omit headingExtent only when the whole source line is heading text. The program checks the span against the source; it never moves an extent or replaces an invalid one with the whole line.
Select the title of the main document and the titles of self-contained documents embedded
later in it. Include every line contributing to each title. A title may consist of the name
of a program, organization or group when that name identifies the content unit starting there.
Select a standalone label at the opening of a table, schedule or other content unit when it
names or introduces that unit. Judge the label separately from the entries below it.
For each target, identify which content unit it names, introduces or divides at this location.
A date, venue, address or meeting-format line may instead describe an attribute of an already
named unit; classify that line separately. Use the source function and the content that follows.
Heading membership includes local titles whether or not they later have outline parents.
Separate a unit's name from its attributes. An occurrence that only states when or where
that already named unit's event takes place, its postal address, or its meeting format is
NOT_SELECT unless it introduces its own distinct content section at that source location.
Those attributes are not contributing title lines merely because they are next to the title
or share its formatting. A day/session divider may include a date while introducing its own
following content; that is a different local function from a date supplied as an event attribute.
For every SELECT, state in reason which content unit the occurrence names, introduces or divides.
In participant or membership lists, a standalone organization or category label introducing
its own following group of entries can be a structural subheading. Evaluate every such peer
label at its own location by the same function: selecting the first subgroup label does not
make the later peer labels ordinary list entries. Individual people, list markers, and an
affiliation supplied as part of one person's entry remain body/metadata. Distinguish a label
naming the following subgroup from an institution merely mentioned within an entry. Compare
the surrounding entries and measured style/block pattern, without letting style alone decide.
