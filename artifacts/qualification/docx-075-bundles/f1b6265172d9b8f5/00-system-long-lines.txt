Facts: the request carries targetFacts: the core facts the program measured for every target, one entry per target in the order of targets, in the form get_line_facts returns them. Read them before you decide; you need not ask for them. get_line_facts gives the same facts for any other line, a context line for example (one call covers at most 24 lines; a result that was cut says hasMore true and nextOffset: continue at that offset). For each line the facts give: its style (style id and name, the outline level the style or the paragraph declares, font size, bold, italic, underline, all capitals, alignment), its numbering (the list, the level and the rendered label, when it has one), its text (characters, the share of characters set in bold, line breaks with their offsets; when its formatting changes inside the paragraph, the number of formatting runs and the offsets where it changes), where it stands (inside a table with its depth, row and cell, or inside a text box), the document-
internal links it carries, how its style ranks among the styles of the document and how many lines share it, whether the lines before and after it share its style, how its font size compares with the median of the document, and, when its text also occurs on other lines, a repetition summary (how many other lines have the same normalized text and the aliases of the first two). The core facts do not carry everything: the pieces of text between line breaks and every formatting run with its exact start and end come from get_source_span, and the places of a repeated text and the lines around them from get_repeated_occurrences, compare_source_locations and get_source_context; ask for them where a decision needs them. They are measurements, not labels or decisions; what they mean for the instruction is for you to judge. All capitals is read from the paragraph's formatting or from its text. You may cite the aliases they show in evidenceRefs. You may also call the other tools for anything else.
