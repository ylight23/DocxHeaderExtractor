[5] L0380:S0: Scanner data is generated by point-of-sales terminals in shops and provides information at the level of the

[R006]
[6] L0381:S0: barcode or, more correctly, Global Trade Item Number (GTIN). Currently, 18 countries are using scanner data

[R007]
[7] L0382:S0: 9

[R008]
[8] L0383:S0: and four are testing their application. The use of scanner data is regulated to ensure data transparency and

[R009]
[9] L0384:S0: allow for price validation.

[R010]
[10] L0385:S0: The reporting of scanner data can be made in two ways: option A) Average price for each GTIN per shop chain

[R011]
[11] L0386:S0: per item (including weights within PPP item); option B: Weighted average price for each PPP item. As for

[R012]
[12] L0387:S0: price weighting, this can also be done by two methods: method 1) weights are available on GTIN level -
[13] L0388:S0: dynamic weighting; method 2) weights are available on the level of the shop chain - fixed weighting. The use
[14] L0389:S0: of scanner data can also be accompanied by traditional price collection, but at the item level it is preferred

[R013]
[15] L0390:S0: to choose either one source or another. From Eurostat member countries’ experience, the use of scanner

[25] L0400:S0: 4. Treatment of Negative Expenditures in ICP

[R020]
[26] L0401:S0: Sergey Sergeev, an independent expert, presented the findings of research on the treatment of basic
[27] L0402:S0: headings with negative expenditure within the PPP aggregation procedures.

[R021]
[28] L0403:S0: The current ICP computation methodology assumes that price data contains only positive values while
[29] L0404:S0: expenditure and quantity data contain only non-negative values (either positive or zero values). However,

[R022]
[30] L0405:S0: GDP expenditures contain several headings where negative expenditure can occur such as: “Balance of
[31] L0406:S0: Exports and Imports”, “Change in inventories”, “Net purchases abroad” and “Acquisitions less disposals of
[32] L0407:S0: valuables”. While cases that affected computation were very limited in the previous ICP exercises, this does

[R023]
[33] L0408:S0: not mean that negative expenditure values have no impact in the calculation of PPPs.

[R024]
[34] L0409:S0: To mitigate the effects of negative headings, two approaches were attempted in the past: i) use of selective
[35] L0410:S0: EKS procedure and ii) use of Tornqvist index for bilateral PPPs instead of Fisher PPPs. However, both methods

[R025]
[36] L0411:S0: are only applicable for the EKS aggregation method. A possible procedure to overcome the issue would be
[37] L0412:S0: to utilize absolute nominal values (for the PPP calculation only) instead of actual nominal values of

[R026]
[38] L0413:S0: expenditure data. This approach is consistent with the theory of the calculation of average indices and is
[39] L0414:S0: applicable not only for the EKS but for other aggregation methods as well.

[R027]
[40] L0415:S0: The TAG noted that the use of absolute nominal values appears to be an improvised approach lacking a clear

[R028]
[41] L0416:S0: theoretical basis, and the discussion focused on alternative treatments for “net exports,” as it is the most
[42] L0417:S0: affected category. Nevertheless, the case was also made that PPP calculations assume non-negative
[43] L0418:S0: expenditures and hopefully distortions caused by negative expenditures are somewhat minimal. It was
[44] L0419:S0: suggested to continue research on this topic in the future.

[45] L0420:S0: 5. Fine Tuning Global Linking Procedures

[R030]
[46] L0421:S0: Robert Hill, University of Graz, presented his research paper on Alternative Global Linking Methods.

[R031]
[47] L0422:S0: The current methods for linking ICP regions at the global level are: i) Region-Product Dummy (RPD) at the

[R032]
[48] L0423:S0: basic heading level and ii) Country Approach with Redistribution (CAR) at the aggregate level. The paper

[R033]
[49] L0424:S0: proposes a third approach that would be applicable at both the basic heading and the aggregate levels and

[R034]
[50] L0425:S0: may be referred as “Geometric Average linking”.

[R035]
[51] L0426:S0: 10

[R036]
[52] L0427:S0: The advantages of Geometric-Averaging are that it treats all regions symmetrically and its logarithmic least

[R037]
[53] L0428:S0: squares property makes it a natural extension of GEKS. However, it is still the case that regions with a higher

[R038]
[54] L0429:S0: number of countries have a larger impact in determining the shadow prices on the product dummies. At the

[R039]
[55] L0430:S0: aggregate level, Geometric-Averaging alters the unfixed GEKS indices by the logarithmic least squares

[R040]
[56] L0431:S0: amount necessary to make them satisfy within-region fixity. This is different from the CAR method, where
[57] L0432:S0: expenditure shares of the regions are the same as in the unfixed global comparison.

[R041]
[58] L0433:S0: On topics for future consideration, it was noted that the problem with GEKS is that it uses bilateral Fishers
[59] L0434:S0: between all possible pairs of countries, which is not necessarily desirable if the data quality of some countries

[R042]
[60] L0435:S0: is less reliable. As an alternative, deleting some of the weakest bilateral pairs or replacing them with chained

[R043]
[61] L0436:S0: indices, may provide better results.

[R044]
[62] L0437:S0: Following on the same issue, Yuri Dikhanov, World Bank, presented the findings of his paper written in
[63] L0438:S0: collaboration with Erwin Diewert, on the topic of “Similarity Linking vs. Multilateral Indices in ICP”.

[R045]
[64] L0439:S0: In this paper the authors compute two “Minimum Spanning Trees” (MSTs) based on two different similarity

[R046]
[65] L0440:S0: indexes. From these calculations it was observed that MSTs and, consequently, MST-linked indices, are highly

[R047]
[66] L0441:S0: dependent on the similarity measure used and may result in significantly different PPPs.

[R048]
[67] L0442:S0: Secondly, the paper compares the results of the CAR method and PPPs from the unrestricted global
[68] L0443:S0: aggregations with 154 countries. The authors observe significant deviations, especially between the Caves,
[69] L0444:S0: Christensen and Diewert (CCD) index (one-stage) and the Éltető - Köves - Szulc (EKS) index (one-stage). As for

[R049]
[70] L0445:S0: the linking factors, it was found that the differences between indexes were not significant, concluding that

[R050]
[71] L0446:S0: the choice for the base country in each region is solid.

[R051]
[72] L0447:S0: Lastly, the authors compare the MSTs to CCD and GEKS. It was observed that the MST-linked Törnqvist indices
[73] L0448:S0: based can differ systematically from each other while deviating further away from the CCD index on average
[74] L0449:S0: more than the GEKS.

[R052]
[75] L0450:S0: Continuing on the topic of fine-tunning global linking procedures, Professor Prasada Rao, presented a
[76] L0451:S0: proposal from the late Professor Alan Heston. Based on his suggestion, global PPPs would be estimated by a
[77] L0452:S0: global CPD, using global core item (GCI) prices only. Fixity would be maintained by combining the
[78] L0453:S0: expenditures and the GCI price levels and non-GCI BHs PLs and expenditures in an EKS aggregation yields

[R053]
[79] L0454:S0: country price levels and GDPs in a common reference currency. Fixity can then be achieved in each region by

[R054]
[80] L0455:S0: distributing the regional total among the countries according to their regional results.

[R055]
[81] L0456:S0: The TAG discussed each presentation separately, but the arguments and conclusions are intertwined given
[82] L0457:S0: that all papers address the same core issue of regional linking. On the issue of regional symmetry, it was

[R056]
[83] L0458:S0: noted that larger regions having a larger impact on global PPPs is not necessarily a distortion and may be in

[R057]
[84] L0459:S0: fact a desirable outcome. The main principle under debate is whether all regions should be treated as equal

[R058]
[85] L0460:S0: parts, regardless of the number of participating economies under each ICP region or should all participating

[R059]
[86] L0461:S0: economies be treated as equals, acknowledging that ICP regions which have larger number of participating
[87] L0462:S0: economies will have a greater impact on global results. Moreover, the current approach of using regional

[R060]
[88] L0463:S0: item lists allows each ICP region to produce regional PPPs that are more representative of their own
[89] L0464:S0: consumption patterns: basing the global comparison on global core items alone would reduce that
[90] L0465:S0: representativity. It was also noted that while there can be advantages in choosing one method over another,
[91] L0466:S0: the methodological consistency and maturity ofthe ICP achieved in recent cycles has enhanced the credibility

[R061]
[92] L0467:S0: and uses of global PPPs. Therefore, while further methodological research is necessary, any changes must be

[R062]
[93] L0468:S0: carefully considered.

[95] L0470:S0: 6. Improving Reliability of Price Comparisons

[R065]
[96] L0471:S0: Professor Erwin Diewert, University of British Columbia and University of New South Wales, presented his

[R066]
[97] L0472:S0: paper on Basic Heading PPPs and the Lack of Matching Problem.

[R067]
[98] L0473:S0: The paper analyses three alternative methods for the calculation of basic heading PPPs at the global level: i)

[R068]
[99] L0474:S0: the Country Product Dummy Method; ii) The Jevons “Star” Country Method and iii) the Jevons-GEKS Method.

[R069]
[100] L0475:S0: In all these methods, missing item prices affects the outcome of basic heading PPPs. The “lack of matching”
[101] L0476:S0: problem occurs when a large portion of items within a basic heading is not priced by two or more regions,
[102] L0477:S0: which affects the reliability of PPPs. The paper showcases this issue by presenting several examples taken

[R070]
[103] L0478:S0: from the ICP 2017 cycle.

[R071]
[104] L0479:S0: An alternative solution would be through similarity linking, choosing five pairs of countries between the five

[R072]
[105] L0480:S0: ICP regions with the highest matching price data between them. The problem is that determining which pairs
[106] L0481:S0: of countries would be optimal for interregional linking is a difficult task.

[R073]
[107] L0482:S0: Yuri Dikhanov, World Bank, presented a paper under the title “Fisher versus Törnqvist when Relative Price

[R074]
[108] L0483:S0: Changes are Large” and the foundation paper “Range of possible results for certain classes of superlative
[109] L0484:S0: price indices” which establishes how superlative indices are different from each other.

[R075]
[110] L0485:S0: The paper argues that the basic aggregation method used in the ICP (the Fisher-based GEKS) is unstable in
[111] L0486:S0: the face of price and expenditure volatility and proposes using the Törnqvist-based CCD (Caves, Christensen
[112] L0487:S0: and Diewert) index instead. The paper analyses both spatial and temporal indices.

[R076]
[113] L0488:S0: For temporal analyses, three types of indices are considered: (i) multilateral, or GEKS (multilateral Fisher) and

[R077]
[114] L0489:S0: CCD (multilateral Törnqvist), (ii) direct, or 2023/1990 direct estimates using only two years, and (iii) chained

[R078]
[115] L0490:S0: indices. These indices are used on two aggregation scopes: (i) over all items, and (ii) over all items without

[R079]
[116] L0491:S0: fast-moving technology items. The fast-moving technology items are the temporal proxies for volatile items

[R080]
[117] L0492:S0: present in the ICP. The results reveal that the chained Törnqvist index has the lowest discrepancies between
[118] L0493:S0: the two aggregation scopes. The paper proceeds to simulate effects of price shocks on indices, showing that
[119] L0494:S0: a price shock always has a larger effect on Fisher compared to Törnqvist indices.

[R081]
[120] L0495:S0: For spatial analysis, the ratio between Fisher and Törnqvist indices is found to be even higher than temporal

[R082]
[121] L0496:S0: analysis. In addition, the GEKS and CCD PPP ratios were also compared for the ICP 2021 results and found

[R083]
[122] L0497:S0: significant differences for some countries. The paper suggest that the Törnqvist-based CCD index is
[123] L0498:S0: preferrable to the Fisher-based GEKS index under the condition of strong price and expenditure shocks.

[R084]
[124] L0499:S0: The TAG discussed each presentation separately, but the main arguments and conclusions between these
[125] L0500:S0: topics naturally overlap. On the issue of missing prices, while they may have adverse effects on PPPs, the
[126] L0501:S0: alternative of pricing every item regardless of its representativeness, introduces other biases. Finding a
[127] L0502:S0: middle ground, where most but not all items are priced, is probably the best option to ensure a meaningful

[R085]
[128] L0503:S0: comparison. On the topic of similarity linking as an alternative to the current global linking approach, the TAG

[R086]
[129] L0504:S0: considered that would be a step back towards the former ring linking approach. Regarding the use of different

[R087]
[130] L0505:S0: indices, it was noted that the main discrepancies in spatial comparisons are attributable to certain regions

[R088]
[131] L0506:S0: which may require further investigation.
